# ADR-006: Bounded Contexts and Service Boundaries

- **ADR ID:** ADR-006
- **Status:** Accepted
- **Group:** Foundational
- **Date:** 2026-08-08
- **Decision Owners:** Architecture Board, CTO

## Context

The AI-Native Autonomous Revenue Employee requires clear foundational direction so that implementation, operations, and future evolution remain coherent. This ADR captures the relevant drivers, alternatives, and consequences for the bounded contexts and service boundaries concern.

## Problem

Multiple foundational options exist for bounded contexts and service boundaries. Without a documented decision, individual teams may choose inconsistent technologies or patterns, creating integration debt, security gaps, and operational sprawl.

## Requirements

- Reflect business domains
- minimize cross-boundary coupling
- support team autonomy

## Decision

Align bounded contexts to business capabilities: Identity, CRM Adapter, AI Core, Mission Control, Communications, and Analytics.

## Alternatives

1. **Horizontal layers organized by technical concern**
2. **Monolithic shared data model**

## Rationale

Business-aligned boundaries reflect domain language, limit scope, and reduce the blast radius of changes.

## Consequences

This decision aligns the foundational domain with the overall architecture. It enables consistency, reduces cross-cutting risk, and leaves room to revisit alternatives when validation data is available.

## Risks

- Boundary disputes
- duplicated concepts

## Mitigations

- Context maps
- ubiquitous language workshops
- architecture board reviews

## Security Impact

By grounding the system in a well-defined layered model, we limit the blast radius of security incidents and keep tenant data within explicitly bounded boundaries.

## Performance Impact

Performance is an emergent property of the foundational boundaries; layer isolation allows independent optimization and caching.

## Scalability Impact

The modular foundation supports both vertical scaling and later decomposition into independently deployable services.

## Cost Impact

Layered modular design reduces rework cost and defers distributed-systems overhead until it is justified by scale.

## Operational Impact

Clear boundaries lower operational complexity by making ownership, monitoring, and failure domains explicit.

## AI Impact

A structured domain model and explicit ports/adapters make AI components portable and easier to evaluate.

## Reversibility

Medium. The decision can be revisited when validation criteria or operational constraints change; alternatives are documented and can be re-evaluated.
