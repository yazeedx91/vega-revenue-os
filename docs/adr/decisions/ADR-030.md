# ADR-030: Human-in-the-Loop and Approval

- **ADR ID:** ADR-030
- **Status:** Accepted
- **Group:** AI
- **Date:** 2026-08-08
- **Decision Owners:** Architecture Board, CTO

## Context

The AI-Native Autonomous Revenue Employee requires clear ai direction so that implementation, operations, and future evolution remain coherent. This ADR captures the relevant drivers, alternatives, and consequences for the human-in-the-loop and approval concern.

## Problem

Multiple ai options exist for human-in-the-loop and approval. Without a documented decision, individual teams may choose inconsistent technologies or patterns, creating integration debt, security gaps, and operational sprawl.

## Requirements

- Protect high-impact actions
- allow autonomy for low-risk work
- provide audit trail

## Decision

Require explicit human approval for high-impact actions, destructive operations, and low-confidence outputs; otherwise allow autonomous execution.

## Alternatives

1. **Fully autonomous with no human review**
2. **Manual approval for every action**

## Rationale

This balance delivers efficiency while satisfying safety, compliance, and customer trust requirements.

## Consequences

This decision aligns the ai domain with the overall architecture. It enables consistency, reduces cross-cutting risk, and leaves room to revisit alternatives when validation data is available.

## Risks

- Latency
- user burden
- unclear approval boundaries

## Mitigations

- Risk scoring
- batch approvals
- clear UX
- policy-driven escalation

## Security Impact

Provider keys, prompt data, and outputs are isolated per tenant; all high-impact actions require explicit approval.

## Performance Impact

Model routing and caching reduce both latency and cost by selecting the smallest capable model for the task.

## Scalability Impact

A multi-provider abstraction and tool registry let us add models and capabilities without core rewrites.

## Cost Impact

Usage is metered per tenant; cheaper models handle routine tasks and expensive models are reserved for complex reasoning.

## Operational Impact

Prompt versioning, model health checks, and fallback chains make AI services observable and maintainable.

## AI Impact

Structured output, confidence scoring, and citations are first-class concerns, directly improving agent reliability.

## Reversibility

Medium. The decision can be revisited when validation criteria or operational constraints change; alternatives are documented and can be re-evaluated.
