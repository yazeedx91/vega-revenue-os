# ADR-093: Third-Party and Vendor Governance

- **ADR ID:** ADR-093
- **Status:** Proposed
- **Group:** Governance
- **Date:** 2026-08-08
- **Decision Owners:** Architecture Board, CTO

## Context

The AI-Native Autonomous Revenue Employee requires clear governance direction so that implementation, operations, and future evolution remain coherent. This ADR captures the relevant drivers, alternatives, and consequences for the third-party and vendor governance concern.

## Problem

Multiple governance options exist for third-party and vendor governance. Without a documented decision, individual teams may choose inconsistent technologies or patterns, creating integration debt, security gaps, and operational sprawl.

## Requirements

- Assess vendors
- approve providers
- plan exit

## Decision

Assess vendor risk, maintain an approved provider list, and have an exit plan for every integration.

## Alternatives

1. **Use any tool without review**
2. **No external vendors**

## Rationale

Vendor governance balances innovation with resilience, security, and compliance.

## Consequences

This decision aligns the governance domain with the overall architecture. It enables consistency, reduces cross-cutting risk, and leaves room to revisit alternatives when validation data is available.

## Risks

- Slow onboarding
- vendor failures

## Mitigations

- Risk tiers
- pre-approved lists
- exit plans

## Security Impact

Governance processes enforce that security, privacy, and compliance are considered in every ADR and feature.

## Performance Impact

Versioned APIs and clear lifecycles let consumers plan migrations, reducing upgrade churn.

## Scalability Impact

Governance by contract lets multiple teams work in parallel without destabilizing shared services.

## Cost Impact

Predictable deprecation and versioning avoid costly fire-drills and backward-compatibility hacks.

## Operational Impact

ADRs, runbooks, and clear ownership reduce tribal knowledge and improve change management.

## AI Impact

Governance defines when an AI may act autonomously and when human approval is mandatory.

## Reversibility

High. The decision can be revisited when validation criteria or operational constraints change; alternatives are documented and can be re-evaluated.
