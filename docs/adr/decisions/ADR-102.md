# ADR-102: Evidence-Backed Deterministic Lead Scoring

- **ADR ID:** ADR-102
- **Status:** Accepted
- **Group:** AI / Intelligence
- **Date:** 2026-08-09
- **Decision Owners:** Architecture Board, CTO

## Context

Lead qualification must be transparent and reproducible. A black-box model score would erode trust and complicate compliance.

## Decision

1. Decompose lead score into `icpMatch`, `signalScore`, `intentScore`, and `evidenceConfidence`.
2. Combine sub-scores using tenant-configurable weights defined in `ICPProfile.scoringWeights`.
3. Use deterministic rules for hard filters (pass/fail) and thresholds for `QUALIFIED`, `NEEDS_REVIEW`, and `NOT_QUALIFIED`.
4. Persist the reason, scores, and evidence references on every `Lead` aggregate.

## Alternatives

1. Use an LLM end-to-end to decide qualification. Rejected: non-deterministic, harder to explain, harder to test.
2. Hide scoring weights. Rejected: tenant-specific ICPs need configurable, visible weights.

## Rationale

Explainable, deterministic scoring is testable, auditable, and aligned with the evidence model.

## Consequences

- Every qualified lead can be traced to specific evidence and weights.
- Score stability is guaranteed by rule-based aggregation.

## Risks

- LLM-derived signal interpretation may still have variance; that variance is captured in per-signal confidence, not the scoring formula.

## Security Impact

No PII is required in scoring; decisions are based on normalized claims.

## Performance Impact

Scoring is in-memory and cheap compared to provider calls.

## Scalability Impact

Score computation is embarrassingly parallel per lead.

## Cost Impact

Low; most cost remains in provider research calls.

## Operational Impact

Operations can adjust ICP thresholds without redeploying.

## AI Impact

Agents supply normalized evidence and signals; scoring is deterministic policy.

## Reversibility

Medium. Changing the scoring formula requires back-testing lead outcomes.
